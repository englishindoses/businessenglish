# Business English Course — Grammar and Language Summary

A record of every grammar point and every key term or phrase taught in the lessons built so far
(Lessons 1–8, plus Review 1, Review 2 and the bonus conversation lesson).

---

## Lesson 1 — Making a Strong First Impression

**Grammar points reviewed:** No explicit grammar focus. Language is presented lexically, through
formality contrast (formal vs. informal register) and fixed phrases for describing a role.

**Key terms and phrases taught**

*Formal introductions*
- It's a pleasure to meet you.
- Allow me to introduce myself.
- How do you do?
- I don't believe we've been introduced.

*Informal introductions*
- Hey, I'm [name]!
- Nice to meet you! I'm in [department].
- So, what do you do here?
- Great to finally put a face to the name!

*Describing your role*
- I'm responsible for... — "I'm responsible for managing client relationships in the APAC region."
- My role involves... — "My role involves coordinating between the design and development teams."
- I work closely with... — "I work closely with the finance department on budget planning."
- I'm based in... — "I'm based in our London office, but I travel to Berlin regularly."
- I've been with the company for... — "I've been with the company for three years now."
- I report to... — "I report to the Head of Operations."

*Showing interest and active listening*
- That sounds interesting! — shows enthusiasm
- Really? Tell me more about that. — invites elaboration
- How did that go? — asks for details
- That must be [challenging / exciting / rewarding]. — shows empathy
- That sounds like a significant undertaking. How's it going?
- How are you finding it so far? Quite a change?
- What kind of product is it, if you don't mind me asking?

*Small talk topics*
- Safe: the weather, recent travel, weekend plans, industry news, the event or conference, hobbies, local restaurants
- Avoid: salary, political opinions, someone's age, religious beliefs, relationship status

## Lesson 2 — Building Professional Relationships

**Grammar points reviewed:** No formal grammar point. Focus is on functional phrase categories and
word order (sentence-building practice with the target phrases).

**Key terms and phrases taught**

*Starting a conversation*
- Mind if I join you?
- I don't think we've met. I'm...
- Is this seat taken?
- Hi, I don't think we've met. I'm [name]. Are you enjoying the event so far?

*Building rapport*
- I remember you mentioned... How's that going?
- That must have been challenging!
- I know exactly what you mean.
- That must be a relief! How long did it take in the end?

*Ending a conversation*
- It's been great chatting. I'd better head off.
- I won't keep you any longer.
- Let's catch up again soon.
- It's been lovely chatting. I'd better head to my meeting, but let's grab a coffee sometime.

*Escaping awkward topics*
- Anyway, how's your project going?
- That reminds me, I meant to ask you...
- That's interesting. Speaking of which...
- That sounds frustrating. Anyway, how's the Henderson project coming along?

*Usage tips*
- Starting: use open questions, not yes/no questions.
- Rapport: reference details and previous conversations.
- Ending: give a reason for leaving and end on a positive note.
- Escaping: acknowledge briefly, then pivot with "anyway" or "speaking of which".

## Lesson 3 — Interview Skills

**Grammar points reviewed:** No standalone grammar rule. Structural focus on the **STAR method**
(Situation, Task, Action, Result) and on cause-and-effect linking.

**Key terms and phrases taught**

*Describing experience*
- I was responsible for...
- One of my key achievements was...
- I successfully managed to...
- I played a key role in...

*Buying thinking time*
- That's a great question.
- Let me think about that for a moment.

*Softening and hedging*
- I would say that...
- I tend to...
- I believe I'm quite good at...
- If I had to choose, I'd say...
- That's something I'm working on improving.

*Structuring and connecting*
- To give you an example...
- This led to...
- As a result...

*Reflection phrases*
- What I learned from that experience was...
- In that situation, I decided to...
- Looking back, I'm proud that...

*STAR method language*
- Situation: "In my previous role at..." / "We were facing a situation where..." / "At the time, the company was..."
- Task: "I was responsible for..." / "My task was to..." / "I needed to..."
- Action: "So I decided to..." / "The first thing I did was..." / "I took the initiative to..."
- Result: "As a result..." / "This led to..." / "The outcome was..."

## Review 1 — Consolidation: Lessons 1–3

**Grammar points reviewed: Verb + -ing vs. verb + to infinitive**

- Verbs followed by **-ing**: enjoy, avoid, finish, consider, suggest, mind, keep, involve
  (e.g. "My role involves managing a team", "Would you mind waiting?")
- Verbs followed by **to + infinitive**: want, need, decide, hope, plan, agree, tend, mean
  (e.g. "I tend to be quite organised", "I meant to ask you about that")
- Special case **stop**: stop + -ing = quit doing something ("I stopped smoking");
  stop + to = pause in order to do something ("I stopped to smoke")
- Tip: many -ing verbs describe feelings or attitudes; many to-infinitive verbs describe intentions or decisions.

**Key terms and phrases reviewed**

- *Describing your role:* I'm responsible for... / My role involves... / I work closely with...
- *Habits and tendencies:* I tend to... / I usually... / I'm quite good at...
- *Highlighting achievements:* I played a key role in... / One of my key achievements was... / I successfully managed to...
- *Showing results:* This led to... / As a result... / The outcome was...
- *Other:* Looking back,... / Something I'm working on... / That reminds me... (changing the topic)

## Lesson 4 — Professional Email Communication

**Grammar points reviewed: Modal verbs for politeness (could / would / might)**

- Could you...? — simple polite request
- Would it be possible to...? — more formal
- I was wondering if you could... — very polite, good for sensitive requests
- I would appreciate it if you could... — polite with a deadline
- Would you mind + **-ing**? — polite for small favours (NOT "Would you mind to...")
- Direct → polite: "Send me the report" → "Could you send me the report?"; "I want a meeting tomorrow" → "Would it be possible to meet tomorrow?"

**Key terms and phrases taught**

*Openings and closings by formality*
- Formal: Dear Mr/Ms [Surname], / I hope this email finds you well. / Kind regards, / Yours sincerely,
- Neutral: Dear [First name], / I hope you're doing well. / Best regards, / Many thanks,
- Informal: Hi [Name], / Hope you had a good weekend! / Thanks! / Cheers,
- Tip: mirror the formality of the person you are replying to.

*Transition phrases and their functions*
- I'm writing to... — stating your purpose
- Following our conversation,... — referring to a previous discussion
- In addition,... — adding more information
- However,... — contrasting or introducing a different point
- Therefore,... — showing a result or consequence
- Please find attached... — mentioning attachments
- Also: I wanted to... / I'm getting in touch about... / Furthermore,... / I should also mention that... / That said,... / On the other hand,... / As a result,... / This means that...

*Email phrasal verbs*
- follow up (on) = contact again about something — "I'm just following up on my previous email."
- put together = create or compile — "I've put together a summary for you."
- fill (someone) in = give someone information they missed — "Can you fill me in on what was decided?"
- keep (someone) posted = continue to give updates — "I'll keep you posted on any changes."
- run (something) by = check with someone before deciding — "I wanted to run this idea by you first."

*Email vs. chat register*
- Email: I wanted to let you know that... / I was wondering if you could... / I'll get back to you... / Thank you for your help with this
- Chat: Heads up / Quick question / Will check and let you know / Thanks!
- Chat shorthand: FYI (for your information), Let me know!, On it!, Makes sense

## Lesson 5 — Phone and Video Call Communication

**Grammar points reviewed: Negative present simple — don't / doesn't + base verb**

- I / you / we / they → **don't** + base verb ("I don't handle client complaints")
- He / she / it → **doesn't** + base verb ("She doesn't manage the overseas accounts")
- After doesn't, always use the base verb — never add -s
- Common errors: "He don't have the report" → "He doesn't have"; "She doesn't has time" → "She doesn't have"

**Key terms and phrases taught**

*Opening calls*
- Good morning, this is [name] from [company].
- I'm calling regarding the proposal we discussed.
- Is this a good time to talk?
- Hi, this is [name] returning your call from earlier.

*Clarifying*
- Sorry, could you repeat that?
- Could you say that again, please?
- Sorry, could you speak up a little?
- Let me just confirm — you said Friday at 3pm?
- So just to clarify, the meeting is on Thursday?
- Could you spell that for me? That's B for Bravo... (phonetic alphabet)

*Technical issues*
- I think there's a delay on the line.
- There seems to be an echo on the line.
- You're breaking up. Let me try reconnecting.
- I can't hear you — you're on mute.
- I think we have a bad connection. Can I call you back?

*Closing calls*
- Thanks for your time today.
- I'll send you a summary by email.
- I'll follow up with an email this afternoon.
- I'll be in touch. Speak soon!

## Bonus Lesson — Time to Recharge (Work-Life Balance)

**Grammar points reviewed: Four key tenses**

1. **Present simple** — habits, routines, general facts: "I usually finish work at 6pm"; "We don't work on weekends."
2. **Past simple** — completed actions at a specific past time: "I visited Paris last month"; "They didn't take any holiday last year."
3. **Present perfect** — life experiences and situations connected to now: "I've worked here for five years"; "She has never been to Japan"; "We've just come back from a two-week vacation."
4. **Future with "going to"** — plans and intentions: "I'm going to take a week off in August"; "She isn't going to work overtime this weekend."

*Error focus:* He don't → He doesn't; She have worked → She has worked; We going to → We are going to;
"I have visited London last March" → "I visited London last March" (past simple with a specific past time).

**Key terms and phrases taught:** topic vocabulary for work-life balance, holidays and free time —
recharge, switch off, burned out, work-life balance, take a day off, mental break.

## Lesson 6 — Speaking Up in Meetings (Part 1)

**Grammar points reviewed: Second conditional for diplomatic suggestions**

- Form: **If + past simple, ... would / could / might + infinitive**
- Direct → diplomatic: "We should postpone the launch" → "If we postponed the launch, we would have more time to prepare."
- "What if we hired more staff? Would that help?"
- "If we changed suppliers, we might get better prices."

**Hedging (softening language)**
- Adverbs: perhaps, possibly, probably, apparently, seemingly
- Phrases: somewhat, to some extent, in a way, more or less
- Verbs: seem, appear, might, could
- Without → with hedging: "This plan won't work" → "This plan might need some adjustments"; "You're wrong about the timeline" → "The timeline could be somewhat optimistic."

**Key terms and phrases taught**

- *Asserting your view:* I firmly believe that... / I'm convinced that... / There's no doubt that...
- *Cautious opinions:* It seems to me that... / I'm inclined to believe... / From my perspective, it seems that...
- *Showing support:* I couldn't agree more. / You make a valid point.
- *Pushing back diplomatically:* I see where you're coming from. My concern is... / I have a slightly different perspective on this. / I take your point. How does that account for...?

*Expertise-based opinions*
- From a [department] perspective, I'm concerned that...
- Based on the data we have, it's clear that...
- In my experience with [area], this approach often...

*Disagreeing without "but"*
- "I agree with you, but I think we should wait" → "I agree with you. My concern is that we might not be ready yet."
- "That's a good idea, but the budget won't allow it" → "That's a good idea. How does that account for the current budget constraints?"

## Lesson 6B — Hedging and Politeness in Meetings

**Grammar points reviewed: The second conditional (extended focus)**

- Form: **If + past simple, ... would / could / might + infinitive**
- The past simple in the if-clause does not refer to the past — it signals that the situation is
  imagined. This psychological distance is what makes the structure diplomatic.
- Examples: "If we moved the deadline back a week, we could test everything properly."

**Four hedging techniques**
1. **Modal verbs** (could, might, would): "That could work, but it might take a bit longer"; "It might be worth revisiting the budget before we commit."
2. **Softening verbs** (seem, feel, tend to): "It seems like...", "I feel like we're rushing this a bit", "I tend to think face-to-face meetings work better."
3. **Qualifying adverbs** (perhaps, probably, maybe, not entirely): "I'm not entirely sure...", "The results are probably going to take longer than we planned."
4. **Softer vocabulary**: concerns instead of problems; room for improvement instead of bad; a few adjustments; a bit off.

**Key terms and phrases taught**
- I was wondering if we could look at the cost breakdown together.
- I'm a bit concerned the timeline might be tight — would it be possible to push it back?
- A couple of the figures on page three seem slightly different from the source data.
- What if we tried doing it weekly instead? It could be interesting.
- If we were to streamline the approval process, it could save the team quite a bit of time.
- I see what you mean, but I actually think the other option would be faster.
- Combining techniques: "I wonder if it might be worth..."

**When to hedge:** more with senior or external audiences, formal or new relationships, and
sensitive topics; less with close colleagues in informal settings, where over-hedging can sound evasive.

## Lesson 7 — Speaking Up in Meetings (Part 2)

**Grammar points reviewed: Reported speech for summarising (tense backshift)**

- Present simple → past simple: "I need more time" → Ana said she needed more time.
- Present continuous → past continuous: "We are working on it" → João said they were working on it.
- Present perfect / past simple → past perfect: "We have finished phase one" → Marina explained that they had finished phase one; "I spoke to the supplier" → Pedro said he had spoken to the supplier.
- will → would: "I will send the figures on Friday" → Pedro said he would send the figures on Friday.
- Professional reporting verbs: mentioned that, pointed out that, explained that, raised the point that, made the point that.

**Key terms and phrases taught**

*Interrupting politely*
- Sorry to jump in — just to build on what you said...
- If I can just jump in here...
- Sorry, can I just pull us back to the main question?
- Sorry to interrupt — can we just stay on [the topic] for a sec?

*Asking for clarification*
- Hang on — what do you mean by that exactly?
- Wait, can you walk me through that again?
- Sorry, could you go over that last part again?
- Can I just check what you mean by that?

*Giving clarification*
- Sure, what I mean is...
- Let me put it another way...
- So for example...
- What I was trying to say is...

*Summarising*
- So, you mentioned that...
- If I understood correctly, you said...
- Just to recap, you pointed out that...
- So what I'm hearing is... Is that fair?
- Are we all on the same page?

## Review 2 — Consolidation: Lessons 4–7

**Grammar points reviewed (all four together)**

1. **Modal verbs for politeness** (Lesson 4): could / would / might + base verb — "Could you send me the report?", "Would you mind forwarding this to the team?", "Would it be possible to reschedule the meeting?"
2. **Negatives in the present simple** (Lesson 5): don't / doesn't + base verb, never adding -s after doesn't — "We don't have the updated contract yet", "She doesn't manage the overseas accounts", "The system doesn't support that feature."
3. **Second conditional for suggestions** (Lesson 6): If + past simple, ... would/could/might + infinitive — "If we offered flexible working hours, we would attract more candidates."
4. **Reported speech for summarising** (Lesson 7): said / mentioned / explained (that) + tense backshift — "He mentioned that they had received over fifty applications."

**Key terms and phrases reviewed**

- *Emails:* I'm just following up on my previous email. / Kind regards, / I hope this email finds you well.
- *Phone and video calls:* I think there's a delay on the line. / Is this a good time to talk? / I didn't quite catch that.
- *Sharing opinions:* I see what you mean, but... / In my opinion... / I couldn't agree more. / I'm not sure I entirely agree. / I have a slightly different view.
- *Interrupting:* Sorry to interrupt, but... / If I could just come in here... / Could I just add something?
- *Making it professional:* "Send me the report" → "I was wondering if you could send me the report"; "I don't understand" → "Could you clarify what you mean?"; "You need to increase the budget" → "If we increased the budget, we could deliver better results."
- *Refocusing:* Could we get back to the main point?

## Lesson 8 — Presenting Ideas with Clarity

**Grammar points reviewed: The passive voice**

- Form: **subject + the correct form of "be" + past participle (V3)**. "Be" carries the tense and
  number (is / are / was / were / has been / have been / will be); the past participle never changes.
- "We conducted a survey" → "A survey was conducted."
- "The team has updated the figures" → "The figures have been updated."
- "We will announce the results on Friday" → "The results will be announced on Friday."
- **When to use it:** to focus on the result rather than the person; when the doer is obvious or unimportant ("268 responses were received"); to sound diplomatic ("A mistake was made"); for formal announcements ("It has been decided that the policy will change in June").
- **Useful passive frames:** It was found that... / It has been shown that... / It should be noted that... / It can be seen that...
- **Caution:** don't overuse it — the active voice shows ownership ("We recommend three changes" vs. "Three changes are recommended").

**Key terms and phrases taught**

*Opening the talk*
- Today I'm going to... (walk you through / talk about / cover / explain...)
- I've divided this into three parts.
- I'll start by..., then I'll move on to..., and finally I'll...

*Signposting*
- Moving on to... (the next point / my second question / the budget / what this means for your team)
- That brings me to...
- To wrap up...

*Emphasising*
- The key takeaway here is...
- What I'd really like to highlight is...
- It's worth noting that...

*Handling questions*
- That's a really good question.
- Let me come back to that in a moment.
- Just to clarify your question — are you asking whether...?
