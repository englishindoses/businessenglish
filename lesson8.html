📖 Reading

Presenting the Employee Engagement Survey

Read the passage and then do the activities below.

Marina (HR):Good morning everyone, and thanks for making the time. Today I'm going to walk you through the results of this year's employee engagement survey.

I've divided this into three parts. I'll start by giving you the headline numbers, then I'll move on to where the surprises were, and finally I'll share some recommendations made by the team.

So, the headline. The survey was sent to all 340 employees, and 268 responses were received over three weeks. The results were then analysed by an external consultancy, so the findings are completely independent. It's worth noting that the response rate — 79% — is the highest we've ever had.

Moving on to the next point — the surprises. Satisfaction with line managers has gone up, but satisfaction with career development has dropped sharply. What I'd really like to highlight is the gap between those two numbers. The key takeaway here is that people feel well supported day to day; they simply can't see where they're going in the long term.

That brings me to our recommendations — but I'm happy to take any quick questions first.

Pedro:Sorry Marina — when you say career development has dropped "sharply", how sharply are we talking?

Marina:That's a really good question. It's down eleven points on last year. Let me come back to that in a moment, when we look at the recommendations.

Ana:And is that across the whole company, or only in certain teams?

Marina:Just to clarify your question — are you asking whether the drop is worse in some departments than in others?

Ana:Exactly.

Marina:Then yes. The drop is concentrated in engineering and customer support. In sales, the score actually went up slightly.

So, to wrap up, three actions have been proposed by the HR team, and I'd like your view on all three before they are taken to the board.

💡 Notice

Marina tells the audience what's coming, signposts every transition, and uses the passive when the focus is on the survey rather than on her team ("268 responses were received", "three actions have been proposed"). She also postpones Pedro's question without dodging it, and checks she has understood Ana's question before answering it.

💡 Flexible frames

Learn "Today I'm going to..." and "Moving on to..." as frames, not as fixed sentences. Many things can follow them.

Today I'm going to walk you through the results... / talk about our hiring plan... / cover three things... / explain why the policy is changing...

Moving on to the next point... / my second question... / the budget... / what this means for your team...

Grammar Review

The Passive Voice for Presentations

The passive voice can be used in presentations to focus on the action itself (e.g. what happened or what was found) rather than on who did the action. It also gives your presentation a more formal, objective tone.

When to use it

  • To focus on the result, not the person: "Engagement scores were improved across the company."
  • When the person is obvious or unimportant: "268 responses were received over three weeks."
  • To sound more diplomatic: "A mistake was made" sounds less accusatory than "Carlos made a mistake".
  • For formal announcements: "It has been decided that the policy will change in June."

Form

subject + the correct form of be + past participle (V3)

The verb be carries the tense and the number, so it changes depending on when the action happened and whether the subject is singular or plural: is / are / was / were / has been / have been / will be. The past participle (V3) itself never changes.

  • We conducted a survey. → A survey was conducted.
  • The team has updated the figures. → The figures have been updated.
  • We will announce the results on Friday. → The results will be announced on Friday.

Useful passive phrases for presentations

  • It was found that career development is our weakest area.
  • It has been shown that regular one-to-ones improve retention.
  • It should be noted that these are preliminary figures.
  • It can be seen that engineering scored lowest of all departments.

💡 Don't overdo it

The passive sounds professional, but too much of it makes you sound distant and bureaucratic. Use the active voice when you want to show ownership: "We recommend three changes" makes it clear who is making the recommendation, while "Three changes are recommended" hides it.

💬 Discussion

Saying What Happened — Without Saying Who

One of the most useful things about the passive voice is that you don't have to specify who did the action. In what kind of situations could this be useful at work?

Look at the examples below. Why is the passive voice being used in these situations?

  • The report was submitted late.
    Instead of: Maria submitted the report late.
  • I was told the meeting was cancelled.
    Instead of: You told me the meeting was cancelled.
  • Some concerns have been raised about the new policy.
    Instead of: Three people in my team complained about the new policy.
