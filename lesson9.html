Lesson 9

Project Updates and Deadlines

Reporting what's done, what's in progress, and what's next, clearly and professionally.

🎯 Objectives

What You'll Learn Today

Grammar Review

Present Perfect Simple vs Continuous

Both tenses connect the past to now, which is exactly what a project update does. The difference is the focus: the simple form talks about the result of a finished activity, and the continuous form talks about an activity still in progress now.

Present perfect simple: focus on the result

have / has + past participle (V3)

  • We've completed the testing phase.
  • We've recorded four of the ten training videos.
  • HR has just sent the new contracts.

Present perfect continuous: focus on the activity in progress

have / has + been + -ing

  • We've been working on the training videos.
  • The team has been interviewing candidates since Monday.
  • The supplier has been having technical problems.

Compare:

  • I've written the report. → It's finished. You can read it now.
    I've been writing the report. → I'm in the process of writing. It's not ready yet.
  • Ana has interviewed six candidates. → Focus on how many candidates she interviewed — the result.
    Ana has been interviewing candidates all morning. → Focus on the duration of the process that is still happening.

The plan from here.
